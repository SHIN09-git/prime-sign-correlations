# Proof and numerical review

This is an author-side review of the preprint by GPT-6.2 Astra and JIN HANZHE. It identifies what each check establishes and the mathematical questions left open.

| Issue | Resolution in the paper |
| --- | --- |
| Prime power in a *linear* zero statistic | Landau's formula already has this property. Ford and Zaharescu discuss it explicitly in Eq. (1.1) of their 2005 paper. Suzuki's integrated explicit formula is another predecessor. The target here is the joint positivity probability of two arithmetic windows. |
| Relation to joint short-interval results | Leung's Theorem 3.1 controls normalized centers up to a power of the inverse width. A second center at a fixed non-unit ratio is of order the entire inverse width. The absolute width of the second window scales with its own center. |
| Frequencies and multiplicities | The zero trace counts a zero of multiplicity $m$ with weight $m$. The corresponding time covariance groups equal frequencies before it squares the weights, producing $m^2$. Section 4 retains this difference and states the exact simplicity criterion. |
| Convergence and passage to an indicator | A Gaussian majorant on a unit time interval and the local zero count bound control partial dyadic blocks in mean square, uniformly in the translate. Passage to a quadrant uses an atomless marginal supplied by one independent phase. |
| Exceptional ordinates | Orthogonality gives a second moment for the exceptional part, with squared multiplicities. Independence of the good block, the centered exceptional vector and a uniform Gaussian Hessian bound make the effect quadratic in the exceptional amplitude. The rational independence property is assumed. |
| Sign and normalization | The atom in the trace has negative sign. A positive prime weight lowers the two-excess probability at its central lag. Inversion applies the negative sine before calibration at 2. At the edge of the window the atom has zero weight. |
| Uniformity in the integer argument | For $n\le 1/(4h)$ each matching window isolates one integer. The smooth trace is $O(h^2\sqrt n)$. The denominator of the calibrated ratio stays positive and the propagated density error is $O(\sqrt N\log(1/h)\eta)$. |
| Order of limits | Infinite-base averaging is performed at fixed width before shrinking the width. Section 15 proves a separate finite-time bound using a finite band and a signed-moment error, and names the extra input needed for a simultaneous asymptotic. |

## Independent numerical checks

* **Canonical spectral kernel (paper §7).** The prime calculation encloses the smooth integral by monotonicity on each side of the tent and enumerates all arithmetic atoms in support. The zero calculation uses a separate summation and an explicit tail bound of less than 0.000954. The independent 192-bit run repeats all root-bracket endpoint signs, the zero count, and doubles the quadrature mesh. Both computations resolve the same sign in each of nine cases.
* **Observed prime counts (paper §8).** A sieve enumerates every prime power used on the two base ranges. An integer event grid locates every count jump. Clipping each cell at its two excess thresholds and integrating with Arb gives enclosures of the finite frequencies. Independent evaluations of the two counts at regularly spaced cell midpoints check the incremental update. The six enclosures are each at most one unit in the ninth decimal place wide.
* **Interpretation.** Ratio 6 has zero limiting first-order coefficient under the independence hypothesis, yet its measured finite frequency crosses one quarter between the two ranges. A computed finite canonical covariance, an actual finite sign frequency, and an asymptotic coefficient are three different quantities.

## First passage, recovery, and finite time

| Question checked | Argument and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Can a composite prime power create an early contact? | Every power counts in the exact experimental statistic. The paper removes powers of exponent at least two only after bounding their local count and the largest weight per candidate. Their product is $o((\log M)/M)$ on the critical scale. |
| Do the two branches open together? | Their exact lower thresholds are $n>M$ and $n>M+1/6$. The offset error in a single overlap is $O(M^{-2})$ and is kept when summing. |
| Is an endpoint of equal masses necessarily the first one? | The limiting argument excludes all contacts to the left of the first positive root by uniformity and positivity. For the strict-overtake convention a finite infimum is an equality by continuity; the same signed limiting bounds apply. Numerical checks exclude earlier events or monotone cells and test both sides of each reported decimal enclosure. |
| Where do the constants enter? | A single affine sign loses one class at 2 and 3, and two classes at larger primes. Its singular series is $4C_2$. The total is $S=8C_2$. The sieve upper bound uses $\log u$ for a short subinterval length $u$, which contributes a factor four on replacing it by $\log M$. |
| Can first-contact data determine a local law? | A limit over all real $M$ allows fixed translations in the scaled variable. The sieve bounds every limiting measure by a constant times Lebesgue measure. The triangular first-moment kernel is one-sided and has a nonzero Fourier multiplier at every real frequency. This forces every limiting density to be constant. A limit along seven numerical heights has no such implication. |
| Does the converse survive exceptional starting heights? | For each finite set of rational translates the shift maps change normalized Lebesgue measure by a vanishing amount. A diagonal argument selects violating centers good at all rational translates. Translation continuity of the ramp extends the identity to every real shift. At a single fixed scaled endpoint, direct integration gives the global dyadic pair count with the explicit factor $z^2/2$. |
| Does one shell suffice for affine tuples? | Integer locking preserves the prescribed offsets. The overlap gives a strictly positive increasing weight; positivity bounds the cumulative count, and the reverse inequality is a genuine contraction. This establishes equivalence even at proposed density zero. |
| Are finite time and shrinking width interchangeable? | The actual remainder includes unbalanced words with zero frequency. The estimate controls a finite signed-word sum at increasing moment orders. A small defect or the stated gap hypothesis then gives the coefficient. Section 15 keeps Lamzouri's effective conjecture separate from Leung's fixed-order condition. |

The six small-width enclosures were reevaluated at 192 bits with an independent count-prefix calculation at both decimal endpoints. ## Formal coverage and remaining questions

The archive contains a clean Lean build, kernel replay and axiom inventory for 35 declarations. The actual one-sided Fourier integral is computed in Lean, including a proof that it is nonzero at every real frequency. Division of smooth compactly supported test functions by a smooth nonzero multiplier is also formalized. The analytic explicit formula, upper-bound sieve, measure-limit converse and finite-band probability arguments are manuscript proofs. The main open arithmetic step is an upper bound at the critical first-passage scale for every large real height. Even that bound would imply the correct order of magnitude as a lower bound for the signed prime-pair count. The predicted constant requires the finer local law of Theorem 4. The dense spectral asymptotic for a common limit of height and width still requires quantitative control of near relations. This review was performed by the authors. Independent specialist assessment remains outstanding.
